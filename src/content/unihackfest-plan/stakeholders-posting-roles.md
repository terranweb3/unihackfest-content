# Key Stakeholders and Posting Roles

## 5.1 Corelia Academy

### Role

Lead organizer, education owner, campaign coordinator, credibility builder.

### Content Direction

Corelia should communicate the strategic vision, education model, partner ecosystem, and impact of UniHackfest.

### Recommended Content Types

- Official campaign announcement
- Thought leadership posts
- University partnership posts
- Sponsor announcement posts
- Course roadmap posts
- Bootcamp recap posts
- Mentor / judge spotlight posts
- Demo Day recap
- Impact report

### Tone

Professional, educational, credible, optimistic, ecosystem-oriented.

### Sample Post Angle

**Title:** Why UniHackfest is more than a hackathon  
**Message:** UniHackfest is designed as a 14-week journey where students learn market insight, AI product building, Rust, Solana, and pitching before entering the competition stage.

---

## 5.2 UniHackfest Official Channels

### Role

Main campaign hub and CTA channel.

### Content Direction

UniHackfest channels should focus on clarity, registration, deadlines, practical guidance, and student-facing communication.

### Recommended Content Types

- Register now / registration opening
- Timeline explainer
- Course roadmap
- FAQ
- Track explanation
- Prize pool announcement
- Deadline reminders
- Qualification round updates
- Demo Day agenda
- Winner announcement

### Tone

Clear, energetic, student-friendly, action-driven.

### Sample Post Angle

**Title:** Your 14-week roadmap to Demo Day  
**Message:** Start with Market Insight, continue with AI Product Building, Rust Survival Kit, Solana Bootcamp, and Pitching — then compete with your project at UniHackfest 2026.

---

## 5.3 UEF / University Faculty Pages

### Role

Academic credibility and official university-level reach.

### Content Direction

University pages should frame UniHackfest as a practical innovation, technology, and career development opportunity for students.

### Recommended Content Types

- Official collaboration announcement
- Campus workshop announcement
- Faculty-supported student opportunity post
- Recap after UniTour session
- Congratulations post for selected teams / winners

### Tone

Formal, academic, student-development focused.

### Sample Post Angle

**Title:** UEF students join UniHackfest 2026  
**Message:** Through UniHackfest 2026, students have the opportunity to learn AI, Web3, product thinking, and practical development skills through a structured learning and competition journey.

---

## 5.4 University Clubs

### Role

Student recruitment, peer-to-peer trust, event energy.

### Content Direction

Clubs should focus on making the event feel accessible, exciting, and relevant to students.

### Recommended Content Types

- Campus workshop invitation
- Why students should join
- Team matching post
- Reminder posts
- Behind-the-scenes stories
- Photo recap
- Student quote cards

### Tone

Friendly, direct, energetic, peer-level.

### Sample Post Angle

**Title:** Looking for your first AI & Web3 hackathon?  
**Message:** UniHackfest is coming to our campus. Join the workshop, find teammates, learn the roadmap, and start building your first real product.

### Recommended Content Types

- Sponsor announcement
- MEXC Ventures track explainer

- Digital asset product ideas
- Winner / finalist shoutout
- Ecosystem support post

## 5.6 Solana

### Role

Technical ecosystem sponsor and developer activation partner.

### Content Direction

Solana content should focus on technical building, devnet deployment, wallet connection, frontend integration, verifiable transactions, and student developer enablement.

### Recommended Content Types

- Sponsor announcement
- Solana track explainer
- Solana devnet checklist
- “What makes a good Solana dApp?” post
- Developer resource post
- Winner / finalist shoutout

### Tone

Technical, builder-first, concise, community-oriented.

### Sample Post Angle

**Title:** Build your first Solana dApp at UniHackfest 2026  
**Message:** Student teams will learn, build, and demo Solana-based products with functional devnet programs, wallet connection, frontend setup, and verifiable transactions.

Supportive, community-oriented, mission-driven.

## 5.8 Mentors / Judges / Speakers

### Role

Expert validation and personal trust.

### Content Direction

Mentors, judges, and speakers should share personal announcements, practical advice, and reflections.

### Recommended Content Types

- “I’m joining UniHackfest as a mentor / judge / speaker”
- Tips for student teams
- What makes a strong hackathon project
- Demo Day reflection
- Winner congratulations

### Tone

Personal, helpful, expert but approachable.

### Sample Post Angle

**Title:** Excited to mentor student builders at UniHackfest 2026  
**Message:** My advice for teams: start with a clear user problem, validate the market insight, then use AI and Web3 only where they create real product value.

---

## 5.9 Student Ambassadors

### Role

Peer-to-peer content, community activation, student-generated momentum.

### Content Direction

Student ambassadors should make UniHackfest feel real, accessible, and worth joining.

### Recommended Content Types

- “I’m joining UniHackfest”
- Looking for teammates
- Learning progress posts
- Campus check-in stories
- Build journey updates
- Final reflection

### Tone

Personal, honest, student-friendly.

### Sample Post Angle

**Title:** I’m joining UniHackfest 2026  
**Message:** I want to try building a real AI & Web3 product, learn with mentors, and join Demo Day with other student teams. If you’re looking for teammates, let’s connect.
